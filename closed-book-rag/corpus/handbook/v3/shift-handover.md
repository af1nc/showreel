# Shift Handover (Handbook v3)

A shift handover is a five minute standing conversation at the cell between the
outgoing shift lead and the incoming shift lead. It is not an email and it is not a
card left on a desk.

## What goes on the handover card

The outgoing shift lead fills in the handover card before the handover conversation
starts. The handover card records every machine running and the job number it is
running, every machine down and the reason it is down, all open fault tags, any
deviation authorised during the shift and who authorised it, the part count against
the plan for the shift, and anything a fresh pair of eyes should watch on the next
shift. Both shift leads sign the handover card at the end of the conversation.

## Things that are never handed over silently

A machine left part way through a setup is never handed over without the setup
sheet marked at the step actually reached. Any deviation authorised during the
shift is repeated out loud during the handover as well as written on the card,
because a deviation that is only written down gets read after it has already caused
a problem. An open fault tag is pointed at, at the machine, not just listed.

If the incoming shift lead has not arrived, the outgoing shift lead hands over to
the cell supervisor in person and does not leave the handover card on the desk.

## Quality holds

Any part or batch on a quality hold is handed over by name and by location, and the
incoming shift lead repeats back where the held material is. Held material that
moves during a shift is the single most common way a hold gets lost.

## Storage

Handover cards are scanned into the shift log at the end of each day and retained
for 12 months. The shift log is the first place an investigation looks, so a card
that says "all fine" and nothing else is not an acceptable handover record.
