# Waste and Solvent Disposal (Handbook v3)

## Segregation at source

Waste is segregated at source into five streams: general waste, dry recyclable,
metal swarf, contaminated absorbent, and solvent. Segregation happens at the cell,
by the person creating the waste, because nobody can segregate it reliably later.
Putting solvent or a solvent soaked rag into any other stream contaminates the
entire container and turns a routine collection into a hazardous one at ten times
the cost.

## Solvent drums

A solvent drum is stored in the bunded solvent store and never in the cell
overnight. A solvent drum in use is kept closed between uses rather than left open
with a funnel in it, and is earthed to the decanting point while it is being
decanted, because decanting solvent generates a static charge. No drum is filled
above 90 percent, to leave room for expansion.

Every solvent drum carries a label naming the contents and the date it was opened.
An unlabelled drum is treated as unknown hazardous waste, which is the most
expensive disposal on the site.

## Metal swarf

Metal swarf is drained of coolant before it goes into the swarf skip. Wet swarf is
rejected by the recycler on arrival and the whole skip is charged back to the site
as mixed waste, so one bucket of undrained swarf costs the site a skip. Swarf from
different material types goes into different skips, and aluminium swarf never goes
into the steel skip.

## Consignment notes

Every waste collection leaves site with a consignment note naming the waste stream,
the quantity, the carrier and the carrier licence number. Consignment notes are
retained for three years and are the only evidence the site has that the waste was
handled properly after it left. A collection with no consignment note is refused at
the gate, whatever has been arranged with the carrier by phone.

## Spills

A solvent spill is contained with the spill kit at the cell before anybody goes to
find a supervisor. Contaminated absorbent from a spill goes into the contaminated
absorbent stream, never into general waste, and the spill is logged as a band three
incident.
