# Returns Handling (Handbook v3)

## Booking a returned part in

A returned part is booked into the quarantine bay the moment it arrives and never
onto the free stock shelves, however obviously good it looks. The returns clerk
records the customer reference, the original despatch note number, the date it came
back, and the reason for return as the customer stated it, in the customer's own
words rather than an interpretation of them.

## Disposition

The quality engineer dispositions every returned part within 5 working days into
one of four outcomes: return to stock, rework, scrap, or return to supplier. A
returned part whose history is unclear is scrapped rather than returned to stock,
because a part with no traceability cannot be sold to anybody else either. A part
returned as damaged is photographed before any rework starts.

## Credit notes

A credit note is raised only after the disposition is recorded, never on receipt of
the part, because a credit raised on receipt cannot be undone when the part turns
out to be undamaged and outside warranty. A credit note above 2000 CU is
countersigned by the commercial manager.

## Customer owned tooling

Customer owned tooling returned for refurbishment is tagged as customer property on
arrival, stored physically separate from company stock in the customer property
area, and is never scrapped, modified or reused without written instruction from
the customer. Customer owned tooling is included in the quarterly stock count as a
separate line, because it is on our site and is not ours.

## Repeat returns

Three returns of the same part number from the same customer inside 90 days is
raised as a quality concern regardless of the disposition of each individual
return, because the pattern is the signal and the individual dispositions hide it.
