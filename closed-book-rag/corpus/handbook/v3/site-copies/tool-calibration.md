# Tool Calibration (Handbook v3)

Every gauge, micrometer, calliper, torque wrench and measuring instrument used to
accept or reject a part carries a calibration label showing its unique asset number
and the date its calibration is due.

## Calibration intervals

A torque wrench is calibrated every 6 months or after 5000 cycles, whichever comes
first, because a torque wrench drifts with use rather than with time. A vernier
calliper or a micrometer is calibrated every 12 months. A surface plate is
calibrated every 36 months. Reference gauge blocks are calibrated every 60 months
and are never taken onto the shop floor, because they are the reference everything
else is checked against.

## An instrument that is out of calibration

An instrument found past its calibration due date is quarantined in the red cabinet
immediately, and the quality engineer reviews every acceptance decision made with
that instrument since its last good calibration. The review happens even when the
instrument is later found to pass, because a passing result today says nothing
about what the instrument was reading last month. Parts accepted on a suspect
instrument are put on hold until the review clears them.

## A dropped instrument

Any instrument that is dropped is out of calibration from the moment it hits the
floor, whatever the date on its calibration label says. A micrometer, a calliper or
a torque wrench that has been dropped on the shop floor goes straight into the red
cabinet and is sent for recalibration. There is no visual inspection shortcut for a
dropped instrument: it does not matter that it looks undamaged, and it does not
matter that it still reads correctly on a test piece. Report the drop even when
nobody saw it happen.

## External calibration

External calibration is placed only with a calibration house holding current
accreditation for the measurement in question, and accreditation for one
measurement is not accreditation for another. Certificates are filed against the
asset number and retained for the working life of the instrument plus three years.
A certificate that does not state the measurement uncertainty is rejected and the
instrument stays quarantined until a complete certificate arrives.

## Personal instruments

Personally owned measuring instruments are not used to accept a part under any
circumstances, because they are not on the calibration register and nobody can say
when they were last checked.
