# Training Records (Handbook v3)

## Competence, not attendance

A training record states what a person is signed off to do, not which course they
attended. Attendance on a course never creates competence on its own. The cell
supervisor signs an operator off as competent after observing them do the job on
the actual machine, and the sign off names the machine class rather than the
department, because competence on one press is not competence on another.

## Refresh intervals

Lockout competence is refreshed every 12 months. Overhead crane operation and
slinging are refreshed every 24 months. First aid is refreshed every 36 months.
A refresh that lapses suspends the sign off on the day it lapses, with no grace
period, and a suspended sign off means the person does not do that job until the
refresh is done.

## Agency and temporary staff

Agency and temporary staff hold exactly the same training records as permanent
staff, and are inducted before their first shift rather than at the end of their
first week. An agency worker with no training record on the system is not counted
in the shift manning plan, because the manning plan is a statement about competent
people and not about bodies.

## The training matrix

The plant manager reviews the training matrix monthly. Any sign off held by fewer
than two people on a machine class that is currently running is flagged as a single
point of failure, and a second person is scheduled for training that month. The
review also lists refreshes falling due in the next 60 days so they are booked
before they lapse rather than after.

## Records and evidence

Training records are held in the training system and retained for six years after a
person leaves. The evidence behind a sign off, meaning the observation notes, is
retained with it. A sign off with no observation record behind it is withdrawn at
the next audit, however experienced the person is.
