# Incident Escalation (Handbook v3)

Status: live. Issued to replace the v2 incident escalation chapter, which is
archived. Escalation is about who is told and how quickly, not about blame.

## Severity bands

Incidents are banded one to four. A band one incident is an injury needing
treatment beyond the first aid room, a fire of any size, or a release of anything
outside the building. A band two incident is a machine event with no injury but
with damage above 5000 CU. A band three incident is a near miss, meaning something
that could have injured somebody and did not. A band four incident is a
housekeeping observation.

## Who is called, and how quickly

A band one incident is called to the duty manager immediately and to the plant
director within 15 minutes, by phone, and the call is made before anybody starts
writing anything down. If somebody is injured badly enough to leave the first aid
room, that is a band one incident and the duty manager is called at once, whatever
time of day it is and whoever is on site.

A band two incident is called to the duty manager before the end of the shift it
happened on. Band three and band four incidents are logged in the incident system
and reviewed at the daily stand up the following morning.

## Stopping the line

The duty manager owns the decision to stop a production line, not the shift lead.
Anybody may stop a machine they are standing at if they believe somebody is about
to be hurt, and nobody is ever questioned for doing so. Stopping the whole line is
a different decision with different consequences, and it sits with the duty
manager.

## The written record

A band one or band two incident needs a written record inside 24 hours, covering
the sequence of events, the immediate actions taken, and the containment applied to
stop it happening again this week. The investigation closes within 10 working days
unless the plant director signs a written extension naming a new date.

## Contractors and visitors

An incident involving a contractor or a visitor is escalated on exactly the same
bands and the same timings as an incident involving an employee, and the host or
the engaging manager is told at the same time as the duty manager.
