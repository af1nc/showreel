# Contractor Permits (Handbook v3)

## The four permit types

Four permits are issued on site: hot work, confined space, work at height, and
electrical isolation. A contractor may need more than one permit for the same job,
and each permit is signed separately by the plant engineer. A permit is issued to a
named person, not to a company.

## Hot work

A hot work permit is valid for one shift only and never rolls over to the next day
or to the next shift. Any hot work that continues tomorrow needs a fresh hot work
permit issued tomorrow. A fire watch is maintained for the duration of the hot work
and for 60 minutes after the last spark, by a person who is doing nothing else.
Combustible material is cleared to 10 metres from the work, or protected with a
fire blanket where clearing it is genuinely impossible.

## Confined space

A confined space permit names a top person who stays outside the space for the
whole duration of the entry and does nothing else. A tested gas meter goes in with
the entrant, and the rescue plan is written before entry rather than improvised
during it. The rescue plan may not rely on the public emergency service as its only
means of recovery, because a rescue that starts with a phone call is not a rescue
plan.

## Work at height and electrical isolation

A work at height permit names the access equipment to be used and the anchor
points. An electrical isolation permit names the circuits isolated, and the lockout
rules in the press line lockout chapter apply in full alongside the permit.

## Closing a permit

The plant engineer closes every permit in person at the work face, not at the desk,
and checks that tools, waste and access equipment have been cleared. A permit left
open overnight is treated as a live hazard: the area stays barriered and the shift
lead is told at handover.
