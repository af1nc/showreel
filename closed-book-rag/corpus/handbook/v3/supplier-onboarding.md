# Supplier Onboarding (Handbook v3)

Status: live. Issued to replace the v2 supplier onboarding chapter, which is
archived.

## The four stages

A new supplier passes four stages before a first purchase order can be raised:
commercial screening, quality assessment, sample approval, and system setup. The
buyer owns commercial screening and system setup. The quality engineer owns the
quality assessment and the sample approval. No stage is skipped for urgency, and a
supplier part way through onboarding is not used "just this once".

## Quality assessment and the score

The quality engineer scores the new supplier against the supplier assessment sheet,
which is scored out of 100. A supplier scoring below 70 fails the quality
assessment outright and cannot be onboarded. A supplier scoring 70 to 84 passes
with conditions, which means a documented improvement plan with named actions and
dates before the first delivery. A supplier scoring 85 or above passes clean.

An on site assessment is mandatory for any supplier of safety critical parts. A
desk assessment is accepted only for catalogue consumables, and a desk assessment
never satisfies the requirement for a safety critical part however good the score.

## Sample approval

Three consecutive sample lots must pass dimensional inspection before sample
approval is given. The quality engineer signs the sample approval record and files
it against the part number, not against the supplier, because approval is for a
part from a supplier and not for the supplier in general. Sample approval lapses
after 18 months with no delivery, and a lapsed approval means three fresh sample
lots.

## System setup

The buyer creates the supplier record, sets the payment terms, and attaches the
signed terms of trade. No purchase order can be raised against a supplier record
that has no terms of trade document attached. A supplier record created for a one
off purchase is still a supplier record and goes through the same four stages.
