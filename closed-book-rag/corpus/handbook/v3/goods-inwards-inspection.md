# Goods Inwards Inspection (Handbook v3)

## On the delivery bay

Every delivery is checked against the purchase order before the driver leaves the
bay: part number, quantity, packaging condition, and the presence of a certificate
of conformity where the part number calls for one. Any discrepancy is written on
the delivery note and photographed before anything is unwrapped further, because
once the packaging is off nobody can tell transit damage from process damage.

## Inspection levels

Catalogue consumables are goods receipt only, with no dimensional inspection.
Standard parts take a sampling inspection at level two. Safety critical parts take
one hundred percent dimensional inspection on the first three deliveries from any
supplier, and revert to level two sampling only when all three deliveries pass
clean. One failure restarts the count of three.

## Rejected material

Rejected material goes to the quarantine bay with a red reject tag showing the
inspector's initials, the date, and the reason for rejection. Rejected material is
never stored in the same rack as accepted stock, even temporarily and even when the
quarantine bay is full: if the bay is full the goods inwards supervisor makes space
rather than parking the reject on a stock rack. The buyer raises a supplier concern
within one working day of the rejection.

## Certificates of conformity

A certificate of conformity that does not carry the heat number or the batch number
is treated as a missing certificate, and the delivery is held until a corrected
certificate arrives from the supplier. A certificate that arrives by email direct
from the supplier is acceptable; one forwarded by a third party is not.

## Urgent deliveries

An urgent delivery gets the same inspection as any other delivery. Material
released to production without inspection needs a written concession from the
quality manager naming the batch and the risk accepted, and a concession is granted
before the material moves, never afterwards.
